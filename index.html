Neuromorphic
Metamaterials

We build computing hardware from the physics of nanoscale magnets, light and electrons, and train the material itself to learn.

Live simulation, running in your browser.

Conventional AI hardware fights its substrate. It demands identical devices, digital precision and an external training loop. We take the opposite route: a physical system's own dynamics, nonlinearity and variability do the computation, and the system is trained in place.

Our working thesis is that device heterogeneity and backpropagation-free learning are computational assets, not defects. We are a group of physicists and engineers in the Department of Physics at Imperial College London.

What we work on

Nanomagnetic computing

Artificial spin ice and nanomagnet arrays used as reconfigurable physical neural networks.

Reconfigurable magnonics

GHz spin waves whose spectrum is programmed by the magnetic state.

Neuromorphic photonics

Random network lasers and magneto-photonic hardware for fast, low-energy vision.

Physical learning

Kolmogorov-Arnold networks built from real devices, trained without backpropagation.
